Four design laws

🔖

The shelf is yours

Your history lives as files first, database second. Export is a feature, not a goodbye gift — ICS, JSON, CSV, one tap, from day one.

why: TV Time deleted every account on shutdown day. Export = the brand.
🙈

Spoiler-safe by default

Stills, synopses, ratings and guest cast blur until you mark the episode watched. No feed reveal can sting you.

why: TV Time's no-spoiler episode comments were its soul; only 2 of 75 clones kept it.
🔔

Notifications that arrive

Per-show FCM topics + device-side rescheduling. Episodes notify on time even when the app was killed — with quiet-hours respect and a daily digest option.

why: refugees' #1 complaint; scores apps bury it under gambling ads.
🕯

No ads, ever

Free core, small fair price at the top. No interstitials, no gambling, no "offers". The interface stays a library, not a slot machine.

why: the missing quadrant — 74/75 clones are free with no revenue plan at all.

Voice

Sound like a librarian, not a barker

TV Time-era users were trained by app marketers to expect confetti. We go the other way: precise, warm, boring in the best sense. Concrete numbers over adjectives.

Identity

Three mark directions

The metaphor is a bookend on a night shelf: dark slab, ember ribbon marking where you stopped. All three are self-drawn (no stock, no trademark tangles). Files live in /design/logo-mark.svg.

A · The Ribbon (primary)

Works at 16 px (favicon) and at billboard size. The ribbon is the product promise: your place, held.

B · Monogram (compact)

For avatars, badges, notification channels. Ribbon keeps the accent in every size.

C · The Row (illustrated)

Spines on a shelf + the glowing "on tonight" dot. For splash screens and store headers, not the icon.

Palette

Ink & ember — a library at night

Dark-first because the product earns its slot at 21:00 on the sofa. Ember is the single loud color (TV Time's yellow progress bar, aged into candle light). One accent per screen, never two.

Motion & feel

Four rules, borrowed from paper

Bookend taps

Every number that matters counts up/down: countdowns tick in mono, never jump. 120 ms/240 ms curves only — nothing bounces, nothing celebrates.

The ribbon drops

When you mark an episode watched, a small ember ribbon falls onto the show's spine. One motion per meaning — no confetti systems.

Page-curl pull-to-refresh

Refreshing tonight's data curls like a page corner (Lottie can fake it; CSS skew suffices). It's the only "fun" gesture we ship.

Silence after 22:00

Motion respects quiet hours the same as notifications: no badges, flashes or staggered animations on the digest screen at night.

System design

One spine, four consumers

The design system sits on the weekend-1 worker without a single schema change. D1 is the single brain; every screen (PWA, Android, widget, calendar app) is a dumb, beautiful reader of the same truth.
